01 · Summary

The idea in three lines

What

An agent that builds KYB evidence packs by buying each registry pull, sanctions screen, news search and model call from competing providers.

For whom

Vendors that sell these packs to the onboarding teams of fintechs and marketplaces, paid per case by card.

Why it's different

It spends more only when the evidence justifies it, and only with the customer's consent and money it can actually fund.

02 · The problem

At a flat price, ignoring a weak match is the profitable choice

Before a fintech or marketplace onboards a new merchant, it must check who owns the business. This is called KYB, "know your business". Many teams buy the evidence from vendors that sell a ready-made pack at a flat price.

The vendor's cost depends on what the first checks find. A simple merchant needs a few cheap lookups. A merchant owned through a holding company needs many more. When a cheap sanctions screen returns a weak match on an owner, resolving it needs a premium screen that costs several times more.

At a flat price, skipping that premium check is the profitable choice, and the customer silently inherits the risk. Nothing today ties together the customer's consent to spend more, per-step buying from competing data and model providers, and the vendor's own limited working capital.

03 · What the agent does

Three jobs, one shared float, every step bought at the live price

  1. 1

    Each customer approves a card hold

    Code quotes the job and the customer approves a hold on their card through Airwallex. The hold is the spending ceiling for that job.

  2. 2

    One deposit per job on Metal

    The agent funds each job from a shared float that is deliberately smaller than the three budgets together.

  3. 3

    Buys every step from three competing providers

    Registry, sanctions screen, news and model calls are paid per request over Metal clearing. The agent reads each provider's live price before paying and refuses anything above the limit.

  4. 4

    Re-routes when a provider slows

    When the cheapest provider slows down, jobs with tight deadlines move to a faster one. Jobs with time to spare stay and save money.

  5. 5

    The AI model judges whether the premium check is worth it

    It reads registry filings and news, for example a note that an owner changed the spelling of his surname, and decides whether a weak match deserves the premium screen. It must quote its evidence word for word, and code checks every quote.

  6. 6

    Reserves the money, then asks the customer

    Code reserves the cost from the float and asks the customer to raise the hold, but never by more than the float can fund. If the customer declines, the pack ships as "possible match, unresolved", never as clear.

  7. 7

    Rations the float, then captures once

    When the float runs short, mandatory checks go first and optional depth last. Each job is charged exactly what it spent plus a disclosed fee, once, never above its hold. The rest of the hold is released and unused deposits are withdrawn.

05 · Demo story

The decision only this agent makes

Three fictional merchants, a synthetic sanctions list, and example prices. Every number on screen will come from one price table.

  1. PlanThree customers approve holds of $14.40, $18.60 and $13.20 as live sandbox pre-authorizations. Each job gets its own deposit on Metal testnet.
  2. RunThree job lanes tick per payment while the price book fills from real provider offers.
  3. New informationJob B's cheap screen returns a weak match (0.46) on a 30% owner, below the band any rule would check.A news snippet says the owner changed the spelling of his surname. The model recommends the premium check and quotes the snippet word for word.
  4. Revised decisionCode prices the follow-up at $4.20, including the kit's 4x longer read, reserves it from the float, and asks to raise B's hold to $20.28.B keeps doing its base work while it waits for approval.
  5. Provider slows 6xJob A moves to the faster provider to meet its deadline. Job C has time, so it stays on the cheap one.
  6. Float runs shortJob C's optional extra depth is refused. The model judges C's pack sufficient, so C ships without it and is charged less.
  7. ResultThe customer approves the raise. The premium screen confirms the match and the case goes to the customer's compliance lead.Replayed on the same data, cheapest-always, a fixed rule and a budget-capped build all miss it.
  8. Live payment faultA real lost answer hits one of B's payments. B's charge locks, the resend pays exactly once, and four records agree to the cent.Final charges: $12.12, $20.18 and $11.06, each under its hold.
06 · Try the logic

How big a raise can the agent ask for?

The agent never asks a customer for more than it can actually spend. It reserves the follow-up from the shared float first, then sizes the new hold from what is deposited plus what is reserved.

New hold = (1 + markup) × (deposited + follow-up) + base fee

New hold asked$20.28+$1.68 on $18.60
Reserved from float$4.20ring-fenced until answered
Free float after$1.00for the other jobs
Ask to raise

The float can fund the follow-up, so the agent reserves $4.20 and asks the customer to raise the hold to $20.28.

Example values. Markup and base fee are open design choices that we will fix after measuring gas on testnet.

07 · What makes it different

Kit 12 with a real buyer and a real decision

A real customer for Kit 12

KYB vendors have exactly the shape the kit describes: per-case budgets, per-step costs that vary, and a premium option that is sometimes worth it.

The model's judgement moves money

It decides when the premium check is worth paying for. We test it on held-out planted cases, including decoys, against simple rules.

Never asks for money it can't spend

The raise is capped by what the shared float can fund, and the reserve is ring-fenced until the customer answers.

Proves it never pays twice

A live lost-answer fault on Metal, a resend that pays once, and four independent records that agree before any charge.

08 · Architecture

Two payment rails, one ledger

Amounts, limits, routing and charges live in code. The AI model only reads filings and news and returns a fixed answer with quoted evidence. It holds no keys and never sets an amount. Sandbox and testnet calls sit behind interfaces so they can be swapped without touching the decision logic.

Safety rules built into the code

  • Never charge above the holdOne capture or one cancel per hold, after reconciliation.
  • Never pay twiceSpend is keyed by invoice id, and nothing is captured while any payment is unresolved.
  • Never label a pack clear with a hit openA declined raise ships as "possible match, unresolved".
  • The model never touches moneyIt cannot import the payment clients, holds no keys and emits no amounts.
  • The float floor is never breachedGas is counted against the same float.
10 · Measuring success

How we'll know it works

On held-out planted cases, the agent must catch hidden matches that simple rules miss, without wasting premium checks on decoys, with zero money-safety violations.
